## Facts (checked 2026-11-06)
- 1925, Paris. Victor Lustig read a newspaper piece about how costly the tower was to maintain. (Wikipedia; Smithsonian)
- A forger made him fake stationery; he posed as Deputy Director-General of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs. (Wikipedia)
- He met scrap dealers at the Hôtel de Crillon, an upscale hotel (most accounts: five or six dealers). Told them the government would sell the tower for scrap, kept secret to avoid public outcry. (Wikipedia; Smithsonian; ISRI)
- He chose André Poisson, an insecure dealer eager to break into Paris business. Convinced him he was a corrupt, underpaid official; Poisson paid a bribe on top of the price. ~70,000 francs. (Wikipedia)
- Lustig fled to Austria. Poisson was too ashamed to go to the police. (Wikipedia)
- Later that year he returned and tried again with new dealers; police were informed; he fled to the U.S. (Wikipedia). We say "tried", not "sold", because sources disagree on whether the second sale completed.
- Died 11 March 1947 at the Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, Springfield, Missouri. Death certificate occupation: "apprentice salesman" (and counterfeiter). (Wikipedia)
- Tower ≈ 7,000+ tons of iron. Built for the 1889 World's Fair; many Parisians called it an eyesore.
