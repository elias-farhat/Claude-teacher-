## Facts (checked 2026-11-06)
- From Aug 25, 1835, the New York Sun (a penny paper founded 1833) ran six articles claiming life on the Moon, falsely credited to astronomer Sir John Herschel and a made-up assistant, "Dr. Andrew Grant". (Wikipedia; Clements Library; Spokesman-Review)
- Herschel really was at the Cape of Good Hope (South Africa) observing the sky. (JSTOR Daily)
- Installments described forests, seas, bison, one-horned blue goats/unicorns, and on day four winged, bat-like humanoids ("Vespertilio-homo") who talked. (Mental Floss; Clements; Wikipedia)
- Other papers reprinted it; it spread across the U.S. and Europe. (JSTOR Daily; Clements)
- The New York Herald exposed it and named Locke; The Sun admitted fabrication Sept 16, 1835. (Spokesman-Review; Wikipedia)
- Author: reporter Richard Adams Locke. Later used "author of the moon hoax" in his byline. (Wikipedia; Mental Floss)
- Herschel, 1837 letter: pestered "from all quarters" about the hoax in English, French, Italian and German. (Clements Library)
- Circulation boost is reported by The Sun itself and "according to legend" — script avoids exact sales numbers.
