TOPIC: What If Your Memory Held a Million Books?

SCRIPT: Monday morning. You sit through a lecture once. You keep every word. Even the professor’s cough... at minute three. Your friend Ben asks about the notes. You recite them. Page by page. Then you reach your front door. Where are your keys? In your hand. Where they’ve been... for ten minutes. Most people hold about four things in mind at once. Maybe seven. Scientists still argue about which. That’s tiny. You? A memory the size of one million books. And one rule. You can never forget... anything. So what if your memory held a million books? Day one. You glance at a phone number. It sticks. You skim a textbook in two minutes. Then you teach it. Ben orders pizza for twelve friends. One time. You repeat the whole order. Perfectly. The waiter applauds. In exams, you write every page you studied. Even where the commas sit. A million books is about eighty billion words. Reading that would take roughly six hundred years. Without sleep. That’s a rough estimate. Your head holds it all. Most people forget much of what they learn within days. Hermann Ebbinghaus showed that back in 1885. You? Nothing slips. In one week, you memorize three medical textbooks. Ben says you’re not a person. You’re a library... on legs. Universities start calling. Thirty offers in one month. But the price arrives quietly. Your mind never filters. Every old embarrassment returns... as if it happened this morning. Every cruel word. Same tone. Sleep slips away. Your brain is two percent of your weight. Yet it burns about twenty percent of your energy. Now it never rests. Psychologist Alexander Luria once studied a man with a famous memory for about thirty years. His memories crowded each other. Abstract ideas got harder. Doctor Hale, your neuroscientist, says something you don’t yet understand. Forgetting isn’t a flaw in the mind... it’s a mercy. Grandma Rose waters her mint on the balcony. She hums a short tune. You’ve known it since childhood. Lately she forgets names. She calls you by your mother’s name. One Friday she asks who you are. You answer gently. Then she asks again. You promise to remember everything for her. You do. She doesn’t. The National Memory Championship. You’re on stage. Three minutes left. Then thirty seconds. The lights are hot. The room is silent. You recite hundreds of lines. Easily. Then your phone buzzes... Ben. Grandma is in hospital. Her fear floods back. Her confusion. Her shaking voice. You can’t forget it. Your power stores everything. It can’t calm. It can’t erase. It can’t rebuild someone else’s memory. Finish and win? Or go to her? You leave the stage. You run. At her bed, you hold her hand. You hum the mint tune... exactly as you heard it. She smiles. Then she says your name. Maya. First time in months. That memory will stay. And you finally get it. Your memory isn’t a burden. It’s a gift... to share. Forgetting isn’t a flaw in the mind... it’s a mercy. So what’s real? In 2006, scientists described a woman who recalls her life in rare detail. But a million books? Impossible. In 1942, Jorge Luis Borges wrote about a man who couldn’t forget. He drowned in detail. Some researchers say forgetting helps us focus. That’s still debated. And sleep locks in what you learn. So never trade it for an exam. That night... you can’t sleep. So what if one hour of sleep felt like eight? What do you think?
