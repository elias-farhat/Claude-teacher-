# Style Bible — "2D cartoon characters in a 3D world" story Shorts

Measured from 8 reference Shorts at 30 fps (every frame), 2026-11-06.
Videos: Grave Bell, Japan Gambling Window, Perfect Railroads, Brain Idea Zone, Storm Oil, Ancient Jar, Pirate Cannonballs, Japan Job Nobody Wants.

**Rules for us:** this is private study. Never copy their scripts, characters, shots or topics. Never name the reference channel. Our stories, characters and voice are always original. We copy the *craft*, not the content.

---

## 1. Format (measured)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Canvas | Vertical 1080×1920 (refs downloaded at 720×1280), 30 fps |
| Length | 39–65 s, median ≈ 62 s |
| Voice | Starts on frame 0. No intro, no logo, no title card |
| Speech pace | ~2.5 caption changes/s (1–2 words each) ≈ 3.5–4 words/s. Fast and steady |
| Audio loudness | −14 LUFS integrated (7 of 8 at −14.0 to −14.3), LRA 1.8–3.5 LU → heavily compressed |
| Music | Continuous bed under the voice. Zero silences longer than 0.25 s in any video |
| Hard cuts | Almost none: 0–4 per video (median 2), and several of those are 1–4-frame flashes |

## 2. Captions

- One word at a time (sometimes 2). Median hold 0.37–0.43 s; 90% of words hold ≤ 0.69 s.
- White, heavy rounded sans (Montserrat Black / similar), thick black outline + soft dark shadow.
- Centered horizontally, baseline at ~70% of frame height (inside the 64–76% band).
- Caption width usually 24–36% of frame width, so it never covers faces.
- Captions are timed exactly to the voice (word timestamps).

## 3. Script formula (all 8 follow it)

1. **Hook = paradox stated as fact, sentence 1, no setup.** 10–16 words.
   Patterns: "They buried people alive with a bell on their finger." / "Gambling is illegal in X, yet…" / "If your ship got hit by X, you'd actually be one of the lucky ones." / "It's impossible to get fired in X, so companies found something worse."
2. **Setting in 1–2 lines:** present tense, concrete details (time of day, a named everyman, a number).
3. **Escalation, 3–6 beats.** Doubters laugh or mock (crew laughing, emperor calling it "the world's most expensive toy", guards joking, boss mocking). Stakes rise each beat.
4. **Mystery beat:** a question or "Wait…?" (about 1 per video).
5. **Pivot: "You see,"** (4 of 8 say it word for word) → the real mechanism, explained with an everyday comparison. Placed either right after the hook (Grave Bell at 3 s, Brain Idea Zone at 4 s) or at ~⅔ of the video as the reveal (Gambling Window at 30 s of 45, Storm Oil at 43 s of 65).
6. **Ironic payoff** in the last 1–2 short sentences. The video ends on the last word; no outro, no "subscribe".

Three modes:
- **Story mode** (5/8): third person, named everyman, present tense (railroad engineer, the cook, the inventor, the widow).
- **"You / Imagine" mode** (2/8): "Imagine you…", the viewer is the main character (Japan job, brain idea zone).
- **Levels mode** (1/8): escalating list, each level worse than the last (pirate cannonballs).

Language: plain spoken English, contractions, short sentences (~13 words), no jargon unless explained in the next line. Always researched real facts.

## 4. Picture

**Characters (2D)**
- Flat 2D cartoon "cards" standing in a 3D world. Egg/bean-shaped heads, cream-white skin, tiny dot eyes or ring eyes, small mouth, often no nose.
- Bodies are capsules/beans with flat color + a soft gradient shade; stick-thin black arms and legs.
- Thick dark plum/black outline, thicker on the shadow side.
- Strong silhouette accessories do the costume work: chef hat, top hat, pirate bandana, helmet, glasses, mustache.
- Emotes: sweat drop, smug half-closed eyes, deadpan, wide-eye panic, laughing crowd.
- Characters take the scene lighting (blue tint at night, warm near lamps), so they look lit, not pasted on.

**World (3D)**
- Stylized 3D sets with hand-painted textures (wood grain, planks, bricks, snow), thin black ink outlines on the geometry.
- Real depth of field: blurred backgrounds, and often a blurred foreground object (porthole frame, shoulder, barrel).
- Particles almost always on: snow, rain, sparks, splinters, smoke, dust, sea spray.

**Color & light (measured)**
- Very saturated: median saturation 0.41–0.62.
- Deep darks: 2nd-percentile luminance 0.03–0.15. Contrast (lum std) 0.17–0.25.
- Shadows tinted purple/plum, never neutral gray: typical shadow RGB (36,25,44), (31,17,32), (45,34,54); night = deep blue.
- Highlights warm cream: ~(220,197,190).
- Warm practical lights (lanterns, candles, lamps, windows) as glowing pools against cool blue ambient. God-rays for big moments.
- **Lighting follows the story:** day → sunset → night → dawn. The darkest stretch usually sits in the 3rd–4th fifth (tension); 5 of 8 videos end at their brightest or near it (sunrise/resolution).

## 5. Camera & editing (measured)

- The camera is almost never still for long. A new camera move starts every **0.9–1.4 s** (median gap ≈ 1.2 s), i.e. roughly every 3–4 words.
- Typical move: ~17 frames (0.55 s), peak ≈ 13 px/frame at 720p. Speed peaks about one-third into the move: quick start, longer soft settle.
- **Scene changes are camera flights, not cuts.** Example (railroads, frame 741): ~10 frames accelerate, a fast glide at 70–85 px/frame along the track, ~10 frames decelerate, landing on the character. Also: whip pans, flying through a window/porthole, following an object (a ball, oil stream, cannonball) from one place to the next.
- Holds have a slow push-in or drift. Low camera heights; dutch tilts for chaos and falls.
- The rare hard cuts are used for a punchline or a 1–4-frame flash on an impact.

## 6. The tiny details that make it fun

1. **Every word is illustrated literally.** When the voice says "garbage", we see garbage; "finger" → close-up on a finger with a string.
2. **In-world labels explain things** instead of graphics: barrel stenciled "LANGRAGE", shop sign "HOT SHOT", crate "CARCASS – KEEP FROM FLAME", can "STORM OIL", death certificate "S. HOLT".
3. **Cutaway views:** X-ray body showing the brain, cross-section of the ground (coffin + string to the bell), inside a cannon barrel, top-down mechanism view, sleep-depth diagram.
4. **Background reactions:** side characters laugh, gossip or panic behind the main action.
5. **Scale gags:** tiny character next to a huge object (1,000-lb cannonball, giant wave).
6. **Running prop/joke** that returns at the payoff (the bell, the shovel, the toy jar, the oil pot).
7. **Ironic final image:** the visual lands the twist (wife calmly sitting with the shovel at sunrise, rival's "perfect" rail buckling).
8. **Constant small motion:** particles, swaying lamps, blinking, the camera drifting. No frame is ever dead.

## 7. Sound

- One narrator, steady confident male voice, slightly playful.
- Music bed throughout (light orchestral/quirky), ducked under the voice; final mix −14 LUFS.
- SFX on every action: impacts, whooshes on camera flights, crowd laughs, splashes, creaks.
- Mix: voice on top, music about −18 to −22 dB under it, SFX punchy but short.
